Part 1: Financial Intuition and Fundamentals
1. Option Profit/Loss and Break-even:
A put option on a share has an exercise (strike) price of |600 and a premium of |25.
(a) Calculate the profit or loss of the put option buyer if the market price at expiry
is: |550, |600, and |650.
(b) Determine the break-even price for the put option buyer.
(c) Now assume instead that the option was a call option with the same strike price (|600)
and premium (|20). Calculate the break-even price for the call option buyer.
2. Currency Arbitrage:
Suppose you can exchange currency A for B at rate 2:1, and B for C at 3:1, and C for A
at 1:8. Is there an arbitrage opportunity? If so, describe a sequence of trades that yields a
profit.
3. Loan Amortization with Equal Monthly Installments:
You take a loan of |50,000 at 12% annual interest, to be repaid in 12 equal monthly installments.

4. Prize Choice Scenario:
You have won a prize! You have two choices:
• Option A: Receive $1 million today.
• Option B: Receive $50,000 at the end of every year, forever (a perpetuity).
Assuming a constant annual interest rate of 4%, which option should you choose and why?
What is the “break-even” interest rate at which you would be indifferent between the two
options?
5. Hedging with Inverse Correlation:
You own 1,000 shares of TechCorp at |200 each (total |2 lakhs). You fear a sector crash.
You notice SafeBank stock moves inversely to TechCorp (when TechCorp falls 10%, SafeBank
rises 5%).
(a) How could you hedge by buying SafeBank shares? Roughly how many shares?
(b) If TechCorp drops 10% (to |180) and SafeBank rises 5%, what is your net position?
(c) What is the main limitation of this hedge compared to using futures or options?
6. Simple Market Making Scenario:
Market maker MM quotes XYZ stock at Bid |50.00 / Ask |50.10 (spread |0.10).
Scenario (over 1 hour):
• 10:00 : Trader A sells 2,000 shares at bid |50.00 → MM buys 2,000 @ |50.00 (pays
|1,00,000)
• 10:15 : Trader B buys 1,500 shares at ask |50.10 → MM sells 1,500 @ |50.10 (receives
|75,150) → MM inventory now: +500 shares
• 10:30 : Trader C sells 800 shares at bid |50.00 → MM buys 800 @ |50.00 (pays |40,000)

• 10:45 : Trader D buys 1,000 shares at ask |50.10 → MM sells 1,000 @ |50.10 (receives
|50,100) → MM inventory now: +300 shares
Calculate:
(a) Total cash flow for MM (money received - money paid)?
(b) Spread profit captured (ignoring inventory)?
(c) Unrealized P&L if XYZ now trades at |49.80?
(d) Was this a profitable hour for MM? Why?

Problem Statement: Build a simple price simulator using Geometric Brownian Motion that
generates multiple possible future stock price paths. Your simulator should:
1. Generate N = 1000 different price paths for a stock over T = 1 year using daily steps
2. Start from S0 = $100 with drift µ = 0.08 and volatility σ = 0.20
3. Plot all 1000 price paths on one graph to see the ”fan” of possible futures
4. Create histogram of final stock prices after 1 year
5. Calculate average final price and standard deviation from simulations
6. Use these price paths to estimate payoff of a simple European call option (strike = $105)
